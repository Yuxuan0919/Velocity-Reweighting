\subsubsection{Population-Level Effective Target with Mass Normalization}

Fix a noisy state $(\mathbf{x}_t,c,t)$ and omit its arguments for notational
simplicity. Let $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{old}}$ denote the velocity field of the
old model, and let $\mathbf{v}_i$ denote the sample-wise flow matching target
associated with generated sample $i$.

We treat the old velocity field as carrying a unit baseline mass. Each generated
sample contributes an additional signed editing mass. For sample $i$, define
\begin{equation}
w_i
=
\beta_a r_i^{+}
-
\beta_d r_i^{-},
\label{eq:net_editing_mass_i}
\end{equation}
where $\beta_a r_i^{+}$ represents the added mass and $\beta_d r_i^{-}$
represents the deleted mass. Thus, $w_i>0$ adds sample mass, whereas $w_i<0$
removes sample mass.

Importantly, the Monte Carlo mass associated with sample $i$ is $w_i$. Therefore, for a batch of $B$ generated samples, the total remaining
mass is
\begin{equation}
M_B
=
1+\sum_{i=1}^{B}w_i
>
0 .
\label{eq:batch_total_mass}
\end{equation}
The positivity condition in Eq.~\eqref{eq:batch_total_mass} ensures that the
mass normalization is well defined.


\paragraph{Mass-Normalized Effective Target.}

After add--delete editing, the unnormalized velocity mass is
\[
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{old}}
+
\sum_{i=1}^{B}
w_i\mathbf{v}_i .
\]

\[
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{old}}
+
\sum_{i=1}^{B}
w_i(\mathbf{v}_i-\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{old}})?
\]
Normalizing it by the total remaining mass gives the finite-sample effective
target
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{B}
=
\frac{
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{old}}
+
\sum_{i=1}^{B}
w_i\mathbf{v}_i
}{
M_B
}
=
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{old}}
+
\frac{
\sum_{i=1}^{B}
w_i
\left(
\mathbf{v}_i-\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{old}}
\right)
}{
M_B
}.
}
\label{eq:batch_mass_normalized_target}
\end{equation}
Hence, the updated target differs from the old velocity field by a signed
mass-weighted residual, normalized by the total remaining mass.


\paragraph{Direct Target-Matching Objective.}

Given the normalized target in Eq.~\eqref{eq:batch_mass_normalized_target}, the
direct target-matching objective is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{target}}
=
\left\|
\mathbf{v}_{\theta}
-
\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{B}
\right\|_2^2 .
}
\label{eq:direct_batch_target_matching_objective}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{target}}
=
\left\|
\mathbf{v}_{\theta}
-
\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{old}}
-
\frac{
\sum_{i=1}^{B}
w_i
\left(
\mathbf{v}_i-\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{old}}
\right)
}{
M_B
}
\right\|_2^2 .
\label{eq:direct_batch_target_matching_expanded}
\end{equation}

This objective is conceptually direct, but it is not directly decomposable into
independent sample-wise squared-error terms. The reason is that the normalized
target contains the batch-level ratio
\[
\frac{
\sum_{i=1}^{B}
w_i
\left(
\mathbf{v}_i-\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{old}}
\right)
}{
M_B
},
\]
which couples all samples before the squared norm is taken.
